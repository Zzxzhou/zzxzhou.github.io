Own-Cluster Reuse Bias in Orthogonal Panel Estimation

Working Paper
[ Abstract ]

Estimating a unit coordinate from the same observations used to evaluate an orthogonal panel score can produce a nonzero asymptotic bias. We study this effect in a partially linear model. For a smooth scalar moment coordinate, the order-$T^{-1}$ score drift has three components: derivative–influence covariance, first-step bias propagation, and curvature. The profiled score makes the first-step-bias component vanish as the nuisance sieve grows. The other two components can shift the $\sqrt{NT}$ limit when $N/T$ converges to a positive constant. We construct feasible bias corrections and standard errors under independent units, deterministic quasi-uniform coordinates, independent uniform covariates, bounded fixed-range dependence, and tensor splines. A regular unique-root result covers smooth scalar moment coordinates; a separate sample-mean result permits a wider sieve window under additional structure. For a sample-mean coordinate and a fixed sieve, buffered splitting removes the covariance component but doubles the leading curvature coefficient under an equal split. Simulations examine both bias mechanisms and feasible inference, including a ratio coordinate with a biased first step.
